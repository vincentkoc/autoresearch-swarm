# Evaluation authority

A successful process produces descriptive measurements. An authorized evaluator
can turn those measurements into an evaluation receipt. Promotion is a separate decision.

Declare `expected_members` in the manifest's evaluation section. This set identifies
the complete evaluation panel. Older manifests still parse, but cannot authorize
evaluation until they declare the panel.

## Procedure

1. Wait for the evaluation job's inputs and dependencies to become available.
2. Call `evaluation.authorize` with its job ID, current revision, and evaluator principal ID.
3. Dispatch the job through its registered worker.
4. Call `evaluation.complete` as the designated evaluator with the request ID and completed attempt ID.

Authorization requires `evaluation.request`, `read`, and `artifact.upload`.
The upload permission covers the immutable input manifest generated by the controller.
Completion requires `evaluation.publish` and `read`. Worker credentials do not grant evaluator authority.

Authorization freezes the input artifact set, experiment digest, seed, metric schema,
expected panel, and evaluator identity before worker admission. It cannot certify an
already admitted or completed attempt. A confirmation request must use a declared
confirmation seed. The statistical plan must additionally govern selection and exposure.

Completion reads the verified report of the accepted attempt. Callers cannot supply
replacement measurements. The service checks the execution identity and records one
receipt per request. Repeated delivery returns that receipt.

An incomplete panel produces an incomplete receipt. Changed metric semantics produce
invalid evidence. Both remain queryable. Neither is a successful scientific comparison.

## Trust boundary

The current profile authenticates the evaluator and binds its report to execution.
It does not isolate hostile training code from all evaluator resources. Protected and
self-reported profiles are not qualified by this service. Trusted local execution
and protected evaluation remain distinct claims.

## Fixed paired gate

`paired-sign-v1` accepts a fixed, ordered panel of independent seed pairs. Define
improvement as comparator minus candidate for a minimized metric. Reverse that
sign for a maximized metric. A win exceeds the declared minimum practical effect.

With `n` pairs and `k` wins, the one-sided binomial tail is
`sum(comb(n, j), j=k..n) / 2**n`. The null hypothesis is
`P(improvement > minimum_effect) <= 0.5`. This is not a confidence interval for
the mean, or the probability that the candidate is better.

The binomial sign-test basis is described in the [NIST sign-test reference](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman1/auxillar/signtest.htm).
This implementation keeps ties as non-wins in the denominator. NIST's usual
sign test excludes ties. Keeping them makes this gate conservative for its stated null.

Promotion requires a complete eligible panel, a tail probability at or below
`1 - confidence`, and a mean improvement above the practical threshold.
An incomplete panel cannot produce a significance result. A complete inconclusive
panel cannot be extended after observing its results.

Five unanimous wins give `1/32`. Three unanimous wins give `1/8`, which cannot
meet a 95% threshold. The policy rejects an infeasible panel before execution.
Exact rational strings preserve probabilities and summaries without JSON integer overflow.

The caller must establish eligibility, pairing, independence, and exposure rules.
Matching seed numbers alone does not establish these assumptions. The pure gate
does not change an incumbent or authorize reuse of a confirmation panel.

## Assessment and selection

`promotion.assess` takes a candidate receipt and the expected incumbent revision.
It records its decision before any incumbent change. The first eligible receipt
can establish an observed baseline. Baseline adoption does not claim improvement.

In observed mode, the comparison uses the incumbent's selected receipt. Callers
cannot choose a weaker historical comparator. A replacement must exceed the
campaign's practical-effect threshold. A paired-confirmation campaign cannot use
this observed replacement path.

`promotion.commit` takes the persisted decision ID. It rechecks eligibility and
the incumbent revision in one transaction. A competing promotion causes a conflict
and leaves the original assessment available for inspection.

Comparisons include the declared protocol, data, evaluator, metric schema, stage
contracts, timing rules, and actual execution fingerprint of every stage. Matching
evaluator hardware cannot hide different training hardware. The current selection
service requires one scoring stage. Multiple scoring stages need an explicit aggregation policy.

## Corrections

`evidence.invalidate` requires separate correction authority. It records the target
and reason, preserving the original run result or receipt. Dependent assessments
become affected. An affected incumbent remains identifiable, with eligibility
suspended and an incremented incumbent revision.

Corrections appear in queries and events. Assessment and commit check their effective
eligibility, even when the original receipt says valid. The service never silently
restores an ancestor. A new selection requires fresh eligible evidence and explicit authority.

Use `promotion.reinitialize` to assess a replacement after suspension. This command
requires its own scope and a trial submitted after the correction. Then use
`promotion.commit` with that assessment. Recovery binds the suspended incumbent
revision and establishes an observed baseline without claiming improvement.

## Confirmation admission

Configure `promotion.mode = "paired_confirmation"`, version `paired-sign-v1`, and
the fixed confirmation seeds when creating the campaign. Allocate an
`evaluation_exposures` budget with kind `exposure` and unit `count`.

After adopting an observed baseline, call `confirmation.create`. Supply the candidate,
validation, evaluator identity, incumbent revision, and execution fingerprint for every stage.
The command creates both sides of every seed pair in one transaction. It also
reserves one evaluation exposure per scoring job. Insufficient trial capacity or
exposure budget rolls back the entire panel.

The first policy allows one confirmation plan per campaign. Ordinary trial submission
cannot use reserved confirmation seeds. The plan cannot attach previously executed
trials, extend its panel, or replace failed realizations with selected retries.

Admission checks the planned execution fingerprint for every stage. Scoring also
requires prior evaluator authorization. The controller derives confirmation exposure
from the sealed trial binding. A caller cannot relabel ordinary evidence as confirmation.

Scoring dispatch transfers its exposure reservation to uncertain access. Native closure
consumes access even if scoring fails. Positive proof that scoring never started
releases the reservation. A failed prerequisite releases an undispatched scoring grant.
Timeout alone cannot release exposure. Execution and exposure use separate settlements.

Call `confirmation.assess` after the panel produces its receipts. While evidence is
pending, it reports the missing trials without calculating significance. Use a new
command key after new evidence arrives. Replaying an old key returns its original response.

A failed required realization makes the complete panel ineligible. Successful panels
produce a persisted gate result and promotion assessment. `promotion.commit` checks
the original incumbent revision and every supporting receipt before selection.

Every pair remains a dependency of the selected result. Correcting either side
suspends an incumbent that depended on that panel. The assessment does not select
the best seed or discard unfavorable replicates.

Confirmed means the specified seed gate passed under the declared contract. It does
not establish generalization or an untouched validation split. Prior development-set
access remains relevant. Creating another campaign does not erase that scientific exposure.
